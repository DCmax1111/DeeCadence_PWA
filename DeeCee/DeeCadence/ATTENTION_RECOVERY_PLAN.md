# DeeCadence Attention Recovery Plan

## Definition of the 88% Target

DeeCadence should not claim that it can medically "fix" attention. The launch target should be operational and measurable:

At least 88% of active 14-day users should improve on one or more attention recovery signals compared with their own baseline.

Core signals:
- Longer longest uninterrupted focus block.
- Higher weekly completed-block count.
- Lower abandoned-session rate.
- Faster return after a paused or saved block.
- Higher post-session self-rated focus.
- More scheduled task units completed through focus blocks.

## Phase 1: Before Launch

Goal: make the first week feel effortless enough that users return.

Build:
- A 60-second baseline check during first run: focus difficulty, usual distraction trigger, preferred session length, current attention confidence.
- Saved focus recovery as a first-class surface in Focus, capped at four incomplete sessions so the user always knows where to continue.
- Adaptive block defaults instead of rigid Pomodoro. Start from user choice, then recommend shorter or longer blocks from completion and fatigue signals.
- A "one block today" entry path that avoids setup fatigue.
- Task splitting into four units so progress is visible even when the full task is not done.
- End-of-block feedback: focus rating, energy rating, distraction reason, and a visible XP gain.
- Reduced-motion support for rank effects and ambient UI motion.

Measure:
- First-session completion rate.
- Day-1 return.
- Saved-session continue rate.
- Median blocks completed per active user.
- Post-session focus rating change from baseline.

## Phase 2: Immediately After Launch

Goal: turn first-use novelty into a repeatable attention habit.

Build:
- A 7-day recovery loop: one daily block, visible streak, and a rank progression moment after completion.
- Self-monitoring cards: "best block this week," "return rate," "most productive time," and "tasks moved forward."
- Gentle prompts and cues based on user intent, not spam: schedule reminders, unfinished saved blocks, and planned task start times.
- Personalized break guidance. If fatigue rises after fixed-timer sessions, recommend self-regulated breaks or shorter blocks.
- Weekly reflection: what helped focus, what interrupted focus, and what to change next week.
- Lightweight feedback capture in-app so beta users can report friction without leaving the flow.

Measure:
- 7-day active retention.
- Week-one improvement rate against baseline.
- Streak survival rate.
- Session abandonment by block number.
- Block length recommendation acceptance.
- Feedback submission rate.

## Phase 3: Months After Launch

Goal: make DeeCadence a credible attention recovery system, not just a timer.

Build:
- A four-week attention recovery program that combines focus practice, planning, environment setup, stress regulation, sleep prompts, and self-regulation skills.
- Adaptive scheduling that learns when the user actually completes deep work and protects those windows.
- Rank progression tuned for long-term effort, with higher ranks requiring substantially more XP and consistency.
- Cohort experiments for block length, break timing, XP reward visibility, saved-session placement, and task-unit design.
- An Attention Recovery Score that combines behavior and self-report without pretending to be a diagnosis.
- Optional phone-away and notification-control rituals before focus starts.

Measure:
- 28-day improvement rate.
- Longest uninterrupted block change.
- Boredom or restlessness self-rating change.
- Planned-task completion rate.
- Saved-session recovery over time.
- Retention by rank and by baseline attention difficulty.

## Evidence Notes

- A 2024 online attention training study found that a four-session program improved attention and reduced boredom experiences among healthy adults.
- A 2025 break-technique study found no overall productivity difference between self-regulated, Pomodoro, and Flowtime breaks, and observed faster fatigue increase under Pomodoro in that sample. DeeCadence should therefore personalize breaks instead of assuming one timer style fits everyone.
- A 2024 systematic review of digital habit formation found common useful techniques such as self-monitoring, goal setting, prompts/cues, and positive reinforcement. DeeCadence already has the right ingredients through XP, scheduling, progress, and saved-session recovery, but they need to be measured and tuned.
